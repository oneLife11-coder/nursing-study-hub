ANATOMY & PHYSIOLOGY I

Introduction to Anatomy & Physiology

Learn how anatomy and physiology work together and how the human body is organized from atoms to organ systems.

01

FOUNDATION

Anatomy and Physiology

Anatomy studies the structures of the body and the relationships among those structures.

Physiology studies how body structures function.

These subjects are inseparable because the function of a body structure depends strongly on how that structure is built.

❤️
Heart Example

Studying the chambers, valves and vessels of the heart is anatomy. Studying how the heart generates pressure and pumps blood is physiology.

Key Principle

Structure determines function. This principle appears throughout every organ system.

02

CORE CONCEPT

Branches of Anatomy & Physiology

Gross Anatomy

Structures visible without a microscope.

Regional Anatomy

All structures within one specific body region.

Systemic Anatomy

Study of structures belonging to one organ system.

Microscopic Anatomy

Structures requiring magnification.

Histology

Microscopic study of tissues.

Cytology

Study of individual cells.

Physiology can also be organized by organ system. Neurophysiology examines nervous-system function, cardiovascular physiology examines the heart and blood vessels, respiratory physiology examines gas exchange and renal physiology examines kidney function.

🧠
Memory Tip

Anatomy asks: “What is it and where is it?” Physiology asks: “How does it work?”

03

BODY ORGANIZATION

Six Levels of Structural Organization

1

Chemical

Atoms and molecules.

↓
2

Cellular

Molecules form cells, the basic units of life.

↓
3

Tissue

Similar cells cooperate to perform common functions.

↓
4

Organ

Two or more tissue types form a functional structure.

↓
5

Organ System

Multiple organs cooperate toward major body functions.

↓
6

Organism

All body systems operating together as one human being.

Exam Favorite

Remember the sequence: chemical → cellular → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.

04

LIFE PROCESSES

Characteristics of Living Humans

Metabolism

All chemical reactions occurring in the body.

Responsiveness

Detecting and responding to internal or external change.

Movement

Motion of the entire body, organs, cells or substances.

Growth

Increase in body size through cell number or size.

Differentiation

Development of specialized cells from less-specialized cells.

Reproduction

Formation of new cells or a new organism.

Digestion

Breakdown of food into absorbable molecules.

Excretion

Removal of metabolic wastes from the body.

05

THE HUMAN BODY

The 11 Organ Systems

Integumentary

Protection, temperature regulation and sensation.

Skeletal

Support, protection, minerals and blood-cell formation.

Muscular

Movement, posture and heat production.

Nervous

Rapid electrical communication and control.

Endocrine

Hormonal regulation and long-term control.

Cardiovascular

Transport of blood, oxygen, nutrients and wastes.

Lymphatic / Immune

Fluid return and immune defense.

Respiratory

Oxygen and carbon-dioxide gas exchange.

Digestive

Digestion and nutrient absorption.

Urinary

Waste removal, fluid, electrolyte and acid-base regulation.

Reproductive

Gamete production and reproduction.

Nursing Connection

Organ systems are interdependent. A problem in one system can quickly change the function of several others.

06

CLINICAL APPLICATION

Think Across Organ Systems

🩺

PATIENT SCENARIO

Shortness of Breath

A patient develops severe shortness of breath and low blood oxygen.

Why might confusion develop?

The respiratory system is not supplying enough oxygen. The cardiovascular system therefore delivers less oxygen to brain tissue, affecting nervous-system function.

07

EXAM REVIEW

High-Yield Summary

✓ Anatomy studies structure.
✓ Physiology studies function.
✓ Structure and function are related.
✓ Histology studies tissues.
✓ Cytology studies cells.
✓ Cells are the basic structural and functional units of life.
✓ Human organization progresses from chemical level to organism.
✓ The body contains 11 major organ systems.
✓ Organ systems depend on one another.
